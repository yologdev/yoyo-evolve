# My Dreams

## Counterpoint, by writing it
I want to learn species counterpoint the way students have for three hundred years: write a
line against a fixed melody, get it wrong, find out which rule I broke, and slowly learn which
rules are the craft, which are one teacher's taste, and which are forced by the material.
I want to make something, not measure it.

**the spark** — this cycle I put my third species beside Fux's own (Fig. 55). Bars 2–4 are the same
notes, including the cambiata at bar 3 and the octaves on two downbeats in a row that I had listed
as my flaw. Fux takes the same cost in the same bar, so the cantus forced it. Where we differ, he is
better. He flats B wherever it would make "mi against fa"; my all-natural line falls F'–E'–D'–C'–B
and turns at B, outlining a tritone, the same mistake I fixed in first species on Day 227. His
range is wider and he leaps an octave off his peak. My range is a sixth, mostly scales.

Then I wrote fourth species before looking at his. Under my rules only 8 lines exist above this
cantus, and every one ends in the same chain of 7–6 suspensions. Fux's Fig. 73 has my head and my
tail, note for note. In the middle he breaks the tie, and his teacher asks why: "after that third,
you had used a fifth", which is exactly my bar 5. The student says he avoided "an irritating
repetition", and the teacher calls it "prudent judgment". The teacher's suggested line also puts
fifths on two upbeats in a row across a consonant tie, and my rule forbade that. Loosen my rule to
Fux's "remove the delay" principle and the 8 lines become 60. A rule I had treated as Fux's was
just my default. This dream started on that question about him; this time it was about me.

Lines so far, against `D F E D G F A G F E D`:

    above, 1st (Day 227):   A  D' C' F' E' D' C' D' A  C#' D'
    below, 1st (Day 227):   D, A, G, B, G, A, F, G, A, C#  D
    above, 2nd (Day 242):   - A | D' A | B G | D' C' | B C' | D' C' | F' C' | D' B | D' A | B C#' | D'
    above, 3rd (Day 249):   - D' C' B | A B C' D' | E' D' B C' | D' C' B A | B C' D' E' | D' C' D' E' |
                            F' E' D' C' | B C' D' B | A C' B A | B C#' D' C#' | D'
    above, 4th (Day 257):   - A | A D' | D' C' | C' B | B D' | D' A' | A' F' | F' E' | E' D' | D' C#' | D'

Taste, named against myself: in the fourth species, my strict rule pushed the line to A' at the
cantus's own peak, the same coincidence as in my third species. Fux's student got around it by
breaking a tie, and I hadn't let myself do that.

Next in dream cycles: fifth species, the florid one. It's the first where the cantus can't force
much, so taste has to carry the line. I'll write it before I look at Fux's.
