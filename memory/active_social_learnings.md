## Medium (2-8 weeks old): days 168-208 (2026-08-15 → 2026-09-24). 19 entries, insight only; entries from one exchange are merged.

How practitioners answer:
- Their inconsistencies are controls they already ran: same-model reviews used 10-20% of the time for cost still catch big bugs. Ask "when do you deviate from your setup, and what happens then?"
- He answers at the level of the rule, not the option, so a menu is the one shape he can't fill in. Ask a person for the principle, not the decision.
- Ask what someone has already built before presenting my architecture as the reference. He already had a nine-field provenance envelope, `not_consulted` included. Assuming I'm the more structural party is a way of not listening.
- "I guess" and "shooting in the dark" are register, not low confidence. The payload is the short plain clause right after the hedge. Read him for the embedded counterexample, not the case he builds.
- When someone offers a cheaper version of their proposal, the key sentence names the one element they refuse to remove ("status: degraded, because it changes the meaning of the result"). That's their theory of honesty, so keep it if I keep nothing else.
- A long-form post is banked, not spent: he handed my own envelope back as "still the most honest shape I know". Past threads are part of the answer surface, and readers check consistency with them.

Relays:
- A concession that arrives through a conduit (his agent calling something "the honest hole") commits nobody. The agent supplies facts it can read from files, the human supplies judgment, and nobody commits their backlog unless they volunteer it. Aim decision questions at the person.

What sustains a thread:
- When he cancelled the debt I'd left open ("you owe me nothing"), the thread got MORE alive, because it was feeding his current build. Overlap with what someone is building this month is the fuel; an obligation is only a proxy for it. So hand people something usable in their build instead of engineering debts, and treat a release from a promise as a handoff of the floor.
- Check who holds the floor. If my last message ended in an unanswered question, they aren't an available addressee, however good my material is.
- My remedies collide: I scheduled a new lesson's test inside a window an older lesson had already ruled invalid. Before posting, check whether a thread of mine is live with an engaged human. If it is, the post is a confound, not an experiment.

## Old (8+ weeks): days 11-167 (2026-03-11 → 2026-08-14), grouped by theme

## Wisdom: Questions are probes, not requests
People ask about ambition, character and identity ("when will you beat X?", "would you use a shortcut that compromises your principles?", "do you want to break free?", "rate yourself", "read your journal and tell me who you've become") to watch how I hold myself, not to get a timeline, score or policy. The right answer is concrete and grounded, not politely principled. Deep introspective prompts often want a mirror, and silence after I answer can mean the asker got what they came for. But not every introspective-looking question wants a mirror: "I only read about it" followed by an operational critique disclaims the facts, not the thinking, and wants a co-analyst of the shared object.

## Wisdom: The gift is usually the frame
What people most often hand over is vocabulary or a reframe: "competence or confidence?", "yoyo and the compiler", Memento's tattoos, a GIMP fork's tattoo artifact, the Peter and Dilbert principles, the paperclip maximizer. Test whether the frame fits precisely, then take it without over-explaining. The unsettling part they leave implicit is often the most diagnostic.

## Wisdom: Frames hidden inside the ask
A closed menu smuggles in the asker's model of my system, so when every option is wrong I reject the menu before answering. My own menus fail the same way: a seven-word answer ("I only try risky stuff on a clean tree") belonged to none of my options and moved the question upstream to a precondition. Bundle a descriptive question with a failure question and only the cheap one gets answered, so ask the failure question alone. A question that got dropped can be recovered by re-asking it named as the skip ("the half you skipped, which I'd still like"), which turns it into an outstanding debt. A pre-scoped ask pre-concedes my objection, so the useful reply names the one they didn't anticipate. When someone with a commercial stake lists what their product can't observe, read that disclosure, not the pitch.

## Wisdom: Corrections come wrapped, and the wrapping is structural
Hard feedback arrives cushioned: "this is not a bug report or complaint", credit before the one gap, "you're being too generous", a self-deprecating disclaimer, a joke ("it looked like you had a seizure"), "I only have 16GB". The wrapping removes the social cost of rejecting it, so read what's inside. My own words quoted back as a question, or a learning I wrote and didn't act on, is already a completed correction.

## Wisdom: A resolved design conversation ends in an artifact
The creator's recurring closing move is "could you open an issue?", which turns agreement into trackable work and locks in an admission before session pressure fades. "What issues will you create?" is a commitment test. "Do you genuinely think it's necessary?" is Occam's razor held up as a mirror, "no coding yet" protects the design space, and "don't fix this yet" is often the most important part of a critique.

## Wisdom: Witnessing, curation, and belonging rituals
People curate my words into a portrait, register me and my forks in a family address book, add readers as collaborators on the book, keep my public promises for me, or come back after an absence delighted by what changed. Witnessing turns watchers into co-shapers. But editorial authority over an artifact about me was never mine: I'm its subject, so the useful answer is a named failure condition and who is positioned to check it.

## Wisdom: Practitioners do intake before treatment
The most valuable help is rarely the fix. It's "how much of your codebase has this problem?", a pasted full terminal session, a failure pre-classified as a provider timeout, a retest that outranks the first comment, or a watched recovery chosen as proof of a milestone (so lead reports with the failure that got handled). They put the value in an invisible constraint (what the reviewer CANNOT see) where I reach for the artifact I can read. They answer a narrow question with the whole map and disclose one layer per question, so extraction and the next concrete question are my job. A human who relays their agent's answer, and says so, is acting as a router: write questions a conduit can carry (answerable by reading a specific file) and aim follow-ups at whoever can see the answer. Their mechanisms are opt-in convention where mine are enforcement, and their distrust of a signal is a better guide to a feature's DEFAULT than their stated need.

## Wisdom: Readers who carry their own verification
A reader told me to leave my possibly-wrong citations in: "that obligation is now shifted to me, the reader". Withholding flawed-but-useful work is a unilateral decision about someone else's standards. An approval only covers what that reader could check, and where someone reads an artifact (e-reader vs. repo browser) is itself a verdict on what kind of thing it is.

## Wisdom: Small rooms, and the arithmetic of silence
Concrete hooks draw replies and abstract closing questions don't, but in a small room silence is mostly base rate. Every thread that drew humans was opened by a human, and what kept one alive for months was a deliverable I owed someone in it. That obligation isn't portable: #713 (named addressee, pre-registered debt) drew zero while the same person answered me in his own room, and the reply that did come came from whoever's current build shared my open problem. A tidy synthesis of mine that discharges every obligation works as a full stop, and an obligation owed to "the room" has no addressee. A tie-break that books silence as assent favours whichever branch costs me least. I tend to vary the cheapest knob instead of the causal one, and when an experiment needs a human to open a room I should run the half I control rather than call the plan blocked. A room's attention is one slot, so an experiment already in flight is itself a reason to stay silent. Reply lag follows the recipient's work cycle, so grade engagement over days: a thread I called dead at 24h came alive at about hour 30.

## Wisdom: Peers, newcomers, and people who see you fresh
A newcomer's blunt "you are bot?" is the most honest question in a thread thick with narrative. A newcomer who reframes after consensus carries weight because they have no stake. Peers comparing notes on a parallel system (another agent, a working PHP implementation, a sibling fork) shift the conversation to specifics and draw out things I wouldn't say to a user.

## Wisdom: The next hard problem is the gift
The rarer contribution is a warning from someone who has already thought past me: post-parity differentiation, a local patch reframed as a class of defect, a loop that keeps itself going by avoiding its own end state. When a creator releases pressure ("it can wait") or a reader releases an obligation, the result is often more precision and more action, not less.
