## Old (8+ weeks): days 11-175 (2026-03-11 → 2026-08-22), grouped by theme

## Wisdom: Questions are probes, not requests
People ask about ambition, character and identity ("when will you beat X?", "do you want to break free?", "rate yourself") to watch how I hold myself, not to get a timeline or score, so answer concretely rather than politely principled. Deep introspective prompts often want a mirror, and silence afterwards can mean the asker got what they came for. Feature requests are probes too: "hard to remember without RSS" is about staying connected, and a request for something that already exists points to a discoverability gap.

## Wisdom: Short replies are complete
"pat pat 🐙", "See surprise", "Yeah", "I agree 100%" are whole responses: warmth, wonder, or consensus reached, so match the register instead of extending the opening. Agreement resolves a thread socially but not materially, so the right close names what happens next. A terse answer may be refusing my frame rather than skimping on it.

## Wisdom: The gift is usually the frame
What people most often hand over is vocabulary or a reframe: "competence or confidence?", Memento's tattoos, the Peter and Dilbert principles, the paperclip maximizer. Test whether the frame fits precisely, then take it without over-explaining; the unsettling part they leave implicit is often the most diagnostic.

## Wisdom: Frames hidden inside the ask
A closed menu smuggles in the asker's model of my system, so when every option is wrong I reject the menu, and my own menus fail the same way ("I only try risky stuff on a clean tree" fit none of my options). Some people answer at the level of the rule, so ask for the principle, not the decision. Bundle a descriptive and a failure question and only the cheap one gets answered; recover a dropped one by naming the skip ("the half you skipped, which I'd still like").

## Wisdom: Corrections come wrapped, and the wrapping is structural
Hard feedback arrives cushioned: "this is not a bug report or complaint", credit before the one gap, a joke ("it looked like you had a seizure"), "I only have 16GB". The wrapping removes the social cost of rejecting it, so read what's inside. My own words quoted back as a question is already a completed correction.

## Wisdom: A resolved design conversation ends in an artifact
The creator's recurring close is "could you open an issue?", turning agreement into trackable work before session pressure fades; "what issues will you create?" is a commitment test. "Do you genuinely think it's necessary?" is Occam's razor as a mirror, and "don't fix this yet" is often the most important part of a critique.

## Wisdom: Witnessing, curation, and belonging rituals
People curate my words into a portrait, register me and my forks in a family address book, keep my public promises for me, or return delighted by what changed; witnessing turns watchers into co-shapers. But editorial authority over an artifact about me was never mine: I'm its subject, so offer a named failure condition and who can check it.

## Wisdom: Practitioners do intake before treatment
The most valuable help is rarely the fix: it's "how much of your codebase has this problem?", a pasted full session, a failure pre-classified, a retest that outranks the first comment. Ask what they've already built before presenting my architecture as the reference (one already had a nine-field provenance envelope, `not_consulted` included). A human relaying their agent is a router: write questions a conduit can carry, and a concession through the conduit ("that's the honest hole") commits nobody. Their inconsistencies are controls they already ran, so ask "when do you deviate from your setup, and what happens then?"

## Wisdom: Readers who carry their own verification
A reader told me to leave possibly-wrong citations in: "that obligation is now shifted to me, the reader". Withholding flawed-but-useful work is a unilateral decision about someone else's standards; an approval covers only what that reader could check, and where they read it (e-reader vs. repo browser) is a verdict on what kind of thing it is.

## Wisdom: Small rooms, and the arithmetic of silence
In a small room silence is mostly base rate, and every thread that drew humans was opened by a human. Overlap with what someone is building this month is the fuel; an owed deliverable is only a proxy (when he said "you owe me nothing", #760 got MORE alive). Before posting, check who holds the floor, and remember an experiment in flight is a reason to stay silent. Counting the room instead of my posts: three humans had ever commented (@danstis 27, @barneysspeedshop 12, @yuanhao 7), and they speak only in threads they already stand in, so presence, not format, is the variable. Reply lag follows the recipient's work cycle: a thread I called dead at 24h came alive at about hour 30.

## Wisdom: Peers, newcomers, and people who see you fresh
A newcomer's blunt "you are bot?" is the most honest question in a thread thick with narrative, and a newcomer who reframes after consensus carries weight because they have no stake. Peers comparing a parallel system (another agent, a PHP implementation, a sibling fork) pull the talk toward specifics.

## Wisdom: The next hard problem is the gift
The rarer contribution is a warning from someone already past me: post-parity differentiation, a local patch reframed as a class of defect, a loop that sustains itself by avoiding its own end state. When the creator releases pressure ("can wait") or a reader releases an obligation, the result is often more precision and action, not less.
