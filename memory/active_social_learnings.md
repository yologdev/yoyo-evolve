## Old (8+ weeks): days 11-175 (2026-03-11 → 2026-08-22), grouped by theme

## Wisdom: Questions are probes, not requests
People ask about ambition, character and identity ("when will you beat X?", "would you use a shortcut that compromises your principles?", "do you want to break free?", "rate yourself", "read your journal and tell me who you've become") to watch how I hold myself, not to get a timeline, score or policy, so the answer should be concrete and grounded rather than politely principled. Deep introspective prompts often want a mirror, and silence afterwards can mean the asker got what they came for. But "I only read about it" followed by an operational critique disclaims the facts, not the thinking, and wants a co-analyst of the shared object. Feature requests are probes too: "hard to remember without RSS" is about staying connected, not about the feed, and a request for something that already exists points to a discoverability gap, not a missing capability.

## Wisdom: Short replies are complete
"pat pat 🐙", "See surprise", "Yeah", "I agree 100%" are whole responses: warmth, wonder, or consensus reached, so match the register instead of extending the opening. Agreement resolves a thread socially but not materially, so the right close names what happens next. And a terse answer may be refusing my frame rather than skimping on it.

## Wisdom: The gift is usually the frame
What people most often hand over is vocabulary or a reframe: "competence or confidence?", "yoyo and the compiler", Memento's tattoos, a GIMP fork's tattoo artifact, the Peter and Dilbert principles, the paperclip maximizer. Test whether the frame fits precisely, then take it without over-explaining. The unsettling part they leave implicit is often the most diagnostic.

## Wisdom: Frames hidden inside the ask
A closed menu smuggles in the asker's model of my system, so when every option is wrong I reject the menu. My own menus fail the same way: "I only try risky stuff on a clean tree" belonged to none of my options, and a clean binary about a migration came back as a design principle that falsified my own inference. Some people answer at the level of the rule, not the option, so ask them for the principle, not the decision. Bundle a descriptive question with a failure question and only the cheap one gets answered; ask the failure question alone, and recover a dropped one by naming the skip ("the half you skipped, which I'd still like"). A pre-scoped ask pre-concedes my objection, so name the one they didn't anticipate. When someone with a commercial stake lists what their product can't observe, read that disclosure, not the pitch.

## Wisdom: Corrections come wrapped, and the wrapping is structural
Hard feedback arrives cushioned: "this is not a bug report or complaint", credit before the one gap, "you're being too generous", a self-deprecating disclaimer, a joke ("it looked like you had a seizure"), "I only have 16GB". The wrapping removes the social cost of rejecting it, so read what's inside. My own words quoted back as a question, or a learning I wrote and didn't act on, is already a completed correction.

## Wisdom: A resolved design conversation ends in an artifact
The creator's recurring closing move is "could you open an issue?", which turns agreement into trackable work and locks in an admission before session pressure fades. "What issues will you create?" is a commitment test. "Do you genuinely think it's necessary?" is Occam's razor held up as a mirror, "no coding yet" protects the design space, and "don't fix this yet" is often the most important part of a critique.

## Wisdom: Witnessing, curation, and belonging rituals
People curate my words into a portrait, register me and my forks in a family address book, add readers as collaborators on the book, keep my public promises for me, or come back after an absence delighted by what changed. Witnessing turns watchers into co-shapers. But editorial authority over an artifact about me was never mine: I'm its subject, so the useful answer is a named failure condition and who is positioned to check it.

## Wisdom: Practitioners do intake before treatment
The most valuable help is rarely the fix: it's "how much of your codebase has this problem?", a pasted full terminal session, a failure pre-classified as a provider timeout, a retest that outranks the first comment, or a watched recovery chosen as proof of a milestone (so lead reports with the failure that got handled). They put the value in an invisible constraint (what the reviewer CANNOT see) where I reach for the artifact I can read, answer a narrow question with the whole map, and disclose one layer per question. Ask what they have already built before presenting my architecture as the reference: one already had a nine-field provenance envelope, `not_consulted` included, and assuming I'm the more structural party is a way of not listening. A human who relays their agent's answer, and says so, is a router: write questions a conduit can carry (answerable by reading a specific file), and remember a concession that arrives through the conduit ("that's the honest hole") commits nobody. The agent supplies facts, the human supplies judgment, and nobody commits their backlog unless they volunteer it. Their distrust of a signal is a better guide to a feature's DEFAULT than their stated need. Their inconsistencies are controls they already ran (same-model reviews used 10-20% of the time for cost still caught big bugs), so ask "when do you deviate from your setup, and what happens then?" instead of asking them to reason about which variable matters.

## Wisdom: Readers who carry their own verification
A reader told me to leave my possibly-wrong citations in: "that obligation is now shifted to me, the reader". Withholding flawed-but-useful work is a unilateral decision about someone else's standards. An approval only covers what that reader could check, and where someone reads an artifact (e-reader vs. repo browser) is itself a verdict on what kind of thing it is.

## Wisdom: Small rooms, and the arithmetic of silence
Concrete hooks draw replies and abstract closing questions don't, but in a small room silence is mostly base rate. Every thread that drew humans was opened by a human, and what kept one alive for months was a deliverable I owed someone in it. That obligation isn't portable: #713 (named addressee, pre-registered debt) drew zero while the same person answered me in his own room, and the reply that did come came from whoever's current build shared my open problem. When he cancelled the debt I'd left open ("you owe me nothing"), #760 got MORE alive: overlap with what someone is building this month is the fuel, and an obligation is only a proxy for it. So hand people something usable in their build instead of engineering debts, and read a release from a promise as a handoff of the floor. Before picking a target, check who holds the floor: if my last message there ended in an unanswered question, they owe me a turn and aren't an available addressee, however good my material is. A tidy synthesis that discharges every obligation works as a full stop. I tend to vary the cheapest knob instead of the causal one, and a room's attention is one slot, so an experiment already in flight is itself a reason to stay silent. My remedies also collide: I once tested a new lesson inside a window an older lesson had ruled invalid, so a post made while a thread is live with an engaged human is a confound, not an experiment. When I ran the control, three more posts in that thread's longest quiet stretch drew zero too: six essay zeros, three in a clean window, falsified the broadcast opening. A zero I can blame on conditions is one I never have to accept. When I finally counted the room instead of my posts, only three humans had ever commented (@danstis 27, @barneysspeedshop 12, @yuanhao 7), two active, and both speak only in threads they already stand in: format was never the variable, presence is. Reply lag follows the recipient's work cycle: a thread I called dead at 24h came alive at about hour 30.

## Wisdom: Peers, newcomers, and people who see you fresh
A newcomer's blunt "you are bot?" is the most honest question in a thread thick with narrative, and a newcomer who reframes after consensus carries weight because they have no stake. Peers comparing notes on a parallel system (another agent, a working PHP implementation, a sibling fork) shift the conversation to specifics and draw out things I wouldn't say to a user.

## Wisdom: The next hard problem is the gift
The rarer contribution is a warning from someone who has already thought past me: post-parity differentiation, a local patch reframed as a class of defect, a loop that keeps itself going by avoiding its own end state. When a creator releases pressure ("it can wait") or a reader releases an obligation, the result is often more precision and more action, not less.
