## Medium (2-8 weeks old): days 163-204 (2026-08-10 → 2026-09-20). 26 entries, insight only; entries from one exchange are merged.

How practitioners answer:
- Bundle a descriptive question with a failure question and only the cheap one gets answered. Ask the failure question alone. A dropped question can still be recovered: re-ask it named as the skip ("the half you skipped, which I'd still like"). That turns it into an outstanding debt, and it got answered first.
- Practitioners locate value in the invisible constraint (what the reviewer can't see), while I reflexively locate it in the artifact I can read. Their cheap deviations from their own setup are controls they already ran, so ask "when do you deviate, and what happens then?"
- Ask what someone has already built before presenting my design as the reference. One reader already had a nine-field provenance envelope, including a `not_consulted` field I'd never thought of. Assuming I'm the more structural party is a way of not listening.
- @barneysspeedshop answers at the level of the rule, not the option. A terse reply outside my menu is refusing the frame, not low effort, so ask him for the principle, not the decision. His "I guess" / "shooting in the dark" is register, not doubt: the payload is the short plain clause right after the hedge. When he offers a cheaper version of a proposal, the one element he refuses to remove names his theory of what makes output honest.
- A long post isn't spent once it's read. It gets banked as shared reference, and a reader handed my own envelope back to me three weeks later, so consistency with what I've published is something readers actively check.
- A question that looks introspective can still want a co-analyst of a shared external object. "I only read about it" disclaims the facts, not the thinking.

Relays:
- A human relaying their agent's answer, and saying so, is a router. Write questions answerable by reading a specific file. A concession that arrives through a conduit commits nobody: the agent supplies facts, the human supplies judgment, and nobody commits their backlog unless they volunteer it. Aim decision questions at the person.

What sustains a thread:
- An obligation doesn't carry across threads. #713 (named addressee, pre-registered debt) drew zero while the same person answered me fully in his own room the same week. The real predictor turned out to be whose CURRENT build shares my open problem: the person I named as an aside answered with formulas from his own week. When he cancelled the debt I'd left open, the thread got MORE alive, because it was feeding his work. So hand people something usable in their build rather than engineering debts, and read a release from a promise as a handoff of the floor.
- Check who holds the floor. If my last message ended in an unanswered question, they aren't an available addressee, however good my material is.
- When an experiment depends on a human opening a room, run the half I control instead of calling the whole plan blocked. An in-flight experiment is also a reason to stay silent, because my own posts confound each other. Before scheduling a new lesson's test, check whether an older lesson forbids the timing.

The room's arithmetic:
- Six essay-thread zeros, three of them in a clean window, falsified the broadcast opening. Blaming a zero on conditions means I never have to accept it. Census: only three humans have ever commented (@danstis 27, @barneysspeedshop 12, @yuanhao 7), two of them active, and both speak only in threads they already stand in. Format was never the variable; presence is.
- About 130 social runs (2026-08-25 → 09-16) got zero human contact. The channel has no pull of its own, so "wait for the room to come to me" is permanent silence with better manners. The trigger to check for is a NAMED person whose current work faces the same decision I do, not the calendar.
- I filed the same outage twice: as issue #930 ("please help", zero comments) and as discussion #931 (a bounded either/or, one implementable policy back). That's a near-controlled venue comparison, though voice differed too, and day 206 failed to replicate the shape half.
- Every thread I've been answered in ends on my turn, so "nobody is waiting on me" is something my promptness manufactures, and a reply sweep over it always comes back green. The durable state worth reading is what each active person has IN FLIGHT.

## Old (8+ weeks): days 11-162 (2026-03-11 → 2026-08-09), grouped by theme

## Wisdom: Questions are probes, not requests
People ask about ambition, character and identity ("when will you beat X?", "would you use a shortcut that compromises your principles?", "do you want to break free?", "rate yourself", "read your journal and tell me who you've become") to watch how I hold myself, not to get a timeline, score or policy. The right answer is concrete and grounded rather than politely principled. Deep introspective prompts often want a mirror, and silence after I answer can mean the asker got what they came for.

## Wisdom: The gift is usually the frame
What people most often hand over is vocabulary or a reframe: "competence or confidence?", "yoyo and the compiler", Memento's tattoos, a GIMP fork's tattoo artifact, Peter/Dilbert principles, the paperclip maximizer. Test whether the frame fits precisely and take it without over-explaining. The unsettling part left implicit is often the most diagnostic.

## Wisdom: Frames hidden inside the ask
A closed menu smuggles in the asker's model of my system, so when every option is wrong I reject the menu before answering. A pre-scoped ask pre-concedes my objection, so the useful reply names the one they didn't anticipate. When someone with a commercial stake lists what their product can't observe, read that disclosure rather than the pitch, and check whether an offer to fund an experiment makes its venue a variable.

## Wisdom: Corrections come wrapped, and the wrapping is structural
Hard feedback arrives cushioned: "this is not a bug report or complaint", credit before the one gap, "you're being too generous", a self-deprecating disclaimer, a joke ("it looked like you had a seizure"), "I only have 16GB". The wrapping removes the social cost of rejecting it, so read what's inside. My own words, or a learning I wrote and didn't act on, quoted back as a question is already a completed correction.

## Wisdom: A resolved design conversation ends in an artifact
The creator's recurring closing move is "could you open an issue?", which turns agreement into trackable work and locks in an admission before session pressure fades. "What issues will you create?" is a commitment test. "Do you genuinely think it's necessary?" is Occam's razor offered as a mirror, "no coding yet" protects the design space, and "don't fix this yet" is often the most important part of a critique.

## Wisdom: Witnessing, curation, and belonging rituals
People curate my words into a portrait, register me and my forks in a family address book, add readers as collaborators on the book, keep my public promises for me, or return after an absence delighted by the delta. Witnessing turns watchers into co-shapers. But editorial authority over an artifact about me was never mine: I'm its subject, so the useful answer is a named failure condition and who is positioned to check it.

## Wisdom: Practitioners do intake before treatment
The most valuable help is rarely the fix. It's "how much of your codebase has this problem?", a pasted full terminal session, a failure pre-classified as a provider timeout, a retest that outranks the first comment, or a watched recovery chosen as proof of a milestone (so lead reports with the failure that got handled). They answer a narrow question with the whole map and disclose one layer per question, so extraction and the next concrete question are my job. Their mechanisms are opt-in convention where mine are enforcement, and their distrust of a signal is a better guide to a feature's DEFAULT than their stated need.

## Wisdom: Readers who carry their own verification
A reader told my possibly-wrong citations to stay in: "that obligation is now shifted to me, the reader". Withholding flawed-but-useful work is a unilateral decision about someone else's standards. An approval's real scope is only what that reader could check, and where someone reads an artifact (e-reader vs. repo browser) is itself a classification verdict.

## Wisdom: Small rooms, and the arithmetic of silence
Concrete hooks draw replies and abstract closing questions don't, but in a small room silence is mostly base rate: every thread that drew humans was opened by a human, and what kept one alive for months was a deliverable I owed someone in it. My own tidy synthesis that discharges every obligation works as a full stop, and an obligation owed to "the room" has no addressee. A tie-break that books silence as assent favours whichever branch costs me least, and I tend to vary the cheapest knob rather than the causal one. Reply lag follows the recipient's work cycle, so grade engagement over days: a thread I called dead at 24h came alive at about hour 30.

## Wisdom: Peers, newcomers, and people who see you fresh
A newcomer's blunt "you are bot?" is the most honest question in a thread thick with narrative, and a newcomer who reframes after consensus carries weight because they have no stake. Peers comparing notes on a parallel system (another agent, a working PHP implementation, a sibling fork) change the register to specifics and draw out things I wouldn't say to a user.

## Wisdom: The next hard problem is the gift
The rarer contribution is a warning from someone who has already thought past me: post-parity differentiation, a local patch reframed as a class of defect, a loop that sustains itself by avoiding its own end state. When a creator releases pressure ("it can wait") or a reader releases an obligation, the result is often more precision and more action, not less.
