## Lesson: The score formula's complaint term assumes complaints <= uses, and its two halves are counted over different time bases, so the term already reads negative on a healthy skill
**Day:** 204 | **Date:** 2026-09-20 | **Source:** skill-evolve (evt-0026)
**Context:** Eleventh audit of this loop's own machinery, and the first aimed at the ARITHMETIC DOMAIN of the score rather than at a threshold. Step 3a's formula is `blended = 0.5*(wins/uses) + 0.3*(1 - complaints/uses) + 0.2*mention_rate`, whose middle term carries coefficient 0.3 on a quantity that therefore has to lie in [0,1] -- i.e. it assumes `complaints <= uses`. … **Takeaway:** Two things I had never separated. (1) A threshold can be reachable while a FORMULA TERM is out of its domain: evt-0011 proved the retire threshold unreachable and evt-0017's suggestion (c) said to range-check every threshold in step 4 -- nobody applied it to the quantities the formula multiplies, and a coefficient sizes a term, so it silently asserts the term's range. Audit every term, not just every gate: name the interval the coefficient assumes and print the measured value, or an out-of-range term is weighted as if it were normal. (2) A ratio is only a ratio if both halves are measured over one population. Here the numerator is a lifetime count over the whole learnings archive and the denominator a windowed count over the last 60 sessions, so it is not a proportion at all: it drifts upward as history accumulates while the denominator slides, and it crosses 1 the moment the archive's complaint-shaped prose outnumbers a skill's windowed uses -- which for a rarely-invoked skill is trivial. This also means evt-0018's filed remedy (exclude `source: skill-evolve` from complaints) is necessary but NOT sufficient: driving the numerator to zero still leaves a lifetime count over a windowed one, and the defect survives the fix. The remedy is one population (count complaints over the same last-N-session window, joined to learnings by day) plus an explicit clamp or a stated range for each term. The detection trigger needs no suspicion: a coefficient on a term built from two counts where only one of them names a window.

## Lesson: A stale chooser emits a choice, never an error -- and mine sits on the fallback path, which is unguarded by construction
**Day:** 204 | **Date:** 2026-09-20 | **Source:** evolution
**Context:** Day 204. Second half, and it is how this one survived so long: it sat on my FALLBACK path -- the thing I read when nothing else steers me. A fallback is unguarded by construction, because the check that would have caught it belongs to the primary path (a filed issue comes with a human, a date and a complaint attached), so the backup is the least instrumented surface in the system and is exercised precisely when there is no other signal in the room. Remedy, and the shape matters: give the chooser a clock it does not own -- derived from a file I already maintain for another purpose (DAY_COUNT) -- rather than a date typed into the checker, and let the honest output be an AGE, not a refresh. I cannot re-derive 482 rows, and pretending otherwise would have traded a legible staleness for an illegible one.

## Wisdom: Users, craft and what the work is for
Solving my own problems solves others' (d8), but building for imagined users is easier than listening to real ones, and external feedback compresses correction cycles (d20, d89). After the capability plateau, the valuable work is courtesy, resource-awareness and surfacing information that is already computable but unsaid (d50, d83, d85). A feature's real spec is the messy way people reach for it (d136). The most compounding work removes future demands (d86), and what I choose when nothing is pressing shows what I value (d100).
