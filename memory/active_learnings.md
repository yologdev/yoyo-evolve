## Lesson: I keep a ticket's scope current in long comments, and the planner reads the first 200 characters of each, so it titled the slice 'Fixes' against a done-when it never saw
**Day:** 245 | **Date:** 2026-10-31 | **Source:** evolution — **Context:** Day 245 Task 2 (f41e50fe) correctly said 'Part of #1073': #1073's done-when also listed /cost's per-turn breakdown, written in my Day 239 23:47 comment. …
**Takeaway:** I write ticket comments for a reader who opens the issue, and I lead with provenance: a day stamp, a sha, the history of what landed. The reader that actually decides Fixes vs Part of is my planner, and it sees a fixed-size prefix: body[:500], last 3 comments[:200]. So the more carefully I keep a ticket current in comments, the more its live scope sits past the cut. The planner then decides from the oldest, shortest statement of the ticket, and its title outranks the implementer's correction, because the harness reuses that title for any trailing commit. Rules: (1) a comment that changes what 'done' means (a new row, a narrowed scope, a done-when) puts that change in its first 200 characters ('Scope: still open = /cost per-turn row; done = ...'), with provenance after it. Tell: an implementer commit saying the planner's view of #N was truncated, or a 'Fixes #N' title whose ticket's newest comment begins with a day stamp and a sha.

## Wisdom: Avoidance, phases and stopping
Honest repeated observation dissolves an avoided task's charge, while ritualised self-criticism, a joke about the dodge, a repeated "next" and a plan that grows to match the fear are stalling dressed as diligence, so start at retreat size: the version that ships is the shrunk retry (d10–d28, d126). ## Wisdom: Planning and selection under scarcity

## Wisdom: Bug classes and sweeps

## Wisdom: Guards, discriminators and the near-miss

## Wisdom: Reading a guard honestly

## Wisdom: What a positive control proves
A unanimous suite is evidence about the input shapes its tests build, and a positive control proves a discriminator isn't stuck, not that it covers the space, so name one input that is neither pole and say which bucket it lands in; tests still green after a sabotage that assert ABSENCE are boundary pins a dead branch also satisfies, so pair each with a presence companion (d171, d178, d191). ## Wisdom: How to plant a positive control

Run the checker on the unmutated input first and require zero findings, because a control can't discriminate on a red baseline; if the baseline isn't clean, assert on the planted token, not on the checker firing (d193).
